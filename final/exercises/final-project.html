Exercise F1: Final Project Proposal (Week 14)

  • Throughout this exercise, I gained valuable experince in applying the theoretical knowledge to a real-world educational setting. Our group proposed an Educational Question and Answering system using the BERT model, aimed at helping students improve their understanding of Machine Learning concepts at their own pace. Turning this idea into a concrete study allowed me to see how classroom theories can be transformed into practical solutions.
  • Based on our experience, one of the challenges that we encountered was collecting sufficient data for our Educational Question and Answering system. From the planning to implementation, we worked trogether to overcome these challenges especially in gathering enough reliable data. We collected information from the module provided by Dr. Rodolfo Raga Jr. and with his approval, we supplemented it through web scraping from credible sources to ensure our dataset was both valid and sufficient.
  • After collecting enough data, organizing the dataset was another crucial step. By structuring our data in Excel with clear columns such as ID, Title, Context, Quiz/Exam Question, and Quiz/Exam Answer, we were able to streamline our workflow. This organized format guided us in implementing the dataset in Google Colab and building the necessary logic for our model.
  • Finally, presenting our study proposal was an eye-opening experience. It helped me build confidence in speaking in front of a large audience, including my professor. Through this process, I discovered my potential for leadership and learned the importance of clear communication, teamwork, and thorough preparation in research-based projects.
  • Based on our Final Project Pipeline, it beings with a Setup Data Preparation where we insert out Webscraped Data and transform it to unstructured educational content. We tackled two significant challenges inherent NLP training, abstract answers and long document contexts—by implementing a dual-strategy preprocessing techniques. First, we integrated a SentenceTransformers to perform semantic alignments, this allowed us to salvage training examples where the answer wasn't an exact substring by locating the most semantically relevant sentence within the context. Second, we applied sliding windows tokenization a 128-token stride, effectively slicing long documents into manageable chunks withoug losing critical information at the boundaries. This rigorously prepared data was then fed into a bert-base-uncased model, fine-tuned within a fully reproducible environment where we locked every random seed to ensure our training results were stable, consistent, and scientifically valid.
  • Beyond thet training phase, we redefined how model performance is measured and delivered to the end-user. Instead of relying solely on standard loss functions, we evaluated our model using what we call the "Holy Trinity" of QA metrics: Exact Match for precision, F1 Score for partial overlap, and Semantic Similarity to capture the true intent behind a predicted answer. This comprehensive evaluation logic serves as the backbone of our deployment layer, a fully interactive Gradio application. In this interface, users can engage in "Quiz" or "Exam" modes across specific topics like Linear Regression or Neural Networks. Crucially, the system acts as an intelligent tutor; it doesn't merely check for keyword matches but embeds the student's input to compare it against the ground truth, offering nuance-aware feedback that recognizes when a student understands a concept even if their wording differs from the textbook.

However, through collaboration and brainstorming with my groupmates, we were able to transform a theoretical research concept into a practical solution for an education problem, specifically to Machine Learning Course. From planning and structuring our approach to executing each step with purposem this experience and learnings allowed me to grow both academically and personally. It marked the beginning of my confidence as leader and strengthened my belief that I am capable of contributing meaningfully to real-world projects.
