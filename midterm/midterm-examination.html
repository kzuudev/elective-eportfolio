Midterm Examination

  • In Midterm Examination, I explained very well my theoretical knowledge about BERT in specific scenario. Starting with demonstrating BERT Framework for pharmaceutical research company to analyze complex clinical trial reports. I start with the Input (Clinical Trial Reports), then preprocessed the text in Text Preprocessing by applying lowercasing, remove special characters, stopword removal and tokenization. This ensures that the text is structured before training the model. I learned that this phase are very crucial for developing an effective BERT.
  • After that, I applied Positional Encoding, allowing the model to read the entire sentence at once while understanding the position and order of each word. Through this step, I realized how important positional information is in helping transformers preserve sentence structure and meaning. Converting tokens into vector representations also taught me how models interpret text numerically, which made me better understand how BERT processes context. Reflecting on this, these concepts became very useful in our final Q&A project because they helped me appreciate how the model identifies answer spans accurately. This experience strengthened my understanding of transformer architecture, which is valuable for future roles in NLP development and machine learning engineering.
  • Moreover, when the tokens passed through the multiple-layer encoder, I learned that this stage is essential for deepening the model’s understanding of language. I realized that this encoder is composed of two important components. The first is the Multi-Head Attention mechanism, which helped me understand how BERT connects relationships between words across the entire sentence, even if they are far apart. After that, the output is refined by the Feed Forward Network, which further strengthens and clarifies these relationships. Understanding this process allowed me to appreciate how BERT builds meaning layer by layer, and this insight also helped me improve my approach in our final project for Question and Answering.
  • After understanding how the encoder works, I also realized why BERT is more suitable for the given scenario compared to traditional machine learning models. BERT captures both the relationship between words and the overall context of the text because it is bidirectional, meaning it reads the text from left to right and right to left at the same time. This helped me understand how BERT forms deeper and more accurate representations of meaning. On the other hand, traditional algorithms usually focus only on surface-level patterns like word frequency or simple matching. As a result, they often fail to capture how words relate to each other, which is a crucial part of our task, the Detection of Dangerous Drug Interactions. Realizing this made me appreciate why advanced language models like BERT are more reliable for context-sensitive and safety-critical applications.
