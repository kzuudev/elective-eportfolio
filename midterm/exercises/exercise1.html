Exercise M1: Exercise M1: Midterm Eportfolio (Week 7)

  • In this exercise about Implementing and Evaluating NLP Application Models using Python, I learned the Natural Language Preprocessing Workflow Pipeline. The process begins with Raw Text, which consists of movie overviews and reviews collected from the datasets. These texts first undergo Text Preprocessing, which includes steps such as lowercasing, and special character removal, stopword removal, and, tokenization to ensure the quality of the data, After preprocessing, different NLP models was trained, including Text Classification, Sentiment Analysis, Part-of-Speech Tagging, and Text Summarization. Finally, the performance of each model was evaluated using the standard metrics such as accuracy, precision, f1-score, and recall and confusion matrix.
  • Moreover, I acquired knowledge in implementation of four NLP task such as Text Classification, Sentiment Analysis, Part-of-Speech Tagging, and Text Summarization. Through the hands-on experience with these tasks, I learned how to preprocess textural data, apply machine learning models, and evaluate their performance across different NLP applications.
  • In Sentiment Analysis, I found this NLP task the easiest to grasp. The objective was to determine the sentiment of a given text, identifying whether the expressed opinion is positive or negative. In this exercise, the model was applied to classify movie reviews based on their sentiment. Reflecting on this, I learned how to preprocess text, handle labeled datasets, and evaluate classification accuracy. This experience strengthened my ability to work with real-world textual data, a skill that is directly applicable to IT roles involving opinion mining, customer feedback analysis, or social media monitoring.
  • I applied models to automatically assign text into predefined categories. Specifically, I classified movie genres (e.g., Action, Adventure, Drama) based on descriptive overviews of the films. Through this exercise, I gained hands-on experience in feature extraction, model training, and evaluating classification performance. Reflecting on this, I realized the importance of accurate labeling and careful preprocessing in achieving reliable results. These skills are highly relevant for IT careers in machine learning, content categorization, and data-driven recommendation systems.
  • In Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging, I learned how to automatically assign each word in a text its corresponding grammatical category (e.g., noun, verb, adjective). For this study, I applied it to descriptive movie texts, helping to analyze sentence structure and understand how words contribute to meaning. Reflecting on this, I realized how fundamental POS tagging is for many NLP tasks, such as parsing, named entity recognition, and syntactic analysis. This experience enhanced my understanding of language structure and strengthened my skills in text preprocessing and linguistic analysis, essential for IT roles involving natural language understanding and advanced text analytics.
